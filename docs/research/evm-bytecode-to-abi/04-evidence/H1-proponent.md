# H1 Proponent Case: Compiler Optimization Dominates ABI Recovery

**SUPPORT STRENGTH: weak — the most direct experimental test of the hypothesis (SigRec, full text) finds that optimization does NOT reduce ABI recovery accuracy; method choice dominates, and multiple approaches (symbolic, neural, and LLM-based) achieve high accuracy on optimized and unoptimized bytecode alike.**

---

## 1. PAPERS THAT SUPPORT THIS HYPOTHESIS

### 1.1 David et al. (2025) — "Decompiling Smart Contracts with a Large Language Model" (full text)
arXiv:2506.19624, no DOI.

This paper provides the strongest *mechanism* evidence for H1. Section 2.3 explicitly names the optimizer's destructive transformations and their consequences:

> "Compiler optimizations further complicate the decompilation process. The Solidity compiler performs various optimizations including constant folding, dead code elimination, and control flow optimization. These transformations can significantly alter the structure of the code, making it difficult to recover the original source patterns. In some cases, multiple different source code patterns might compile to identical or very similar bytecode, creating ambiguity in the decompilation process."

**Specific findings:**
- The non-injectivity claim (multiple source patterns → identical bytecode) is stated as fact.
- Entropy analysis (§8.1): Solidity source averages 4.22 bits/token vs. EVM bytecode 6.30 bits/opcode. This 50% information-density increase reflects the compression that destroys high-level structural artifacts.
- The system's semantic similarity of 0.82 average (78.3% above 0.8) means approximately 18% of semantic content is unrecoverable — consistent with an information-loss ceiling.
- Test set: 9,731 smart contract functions held out from 238,446 training pairs.
- Even with domain-specific fine-tuning (LoRA on Llama-3.2-3B), the model cannot perfectly recover all contracts.

**Quality assessment:** Moderate. The paper directly states the mechanism H1 relies on (non-injective transformations, structural alteration). Its entropy measurements provide an information-theoretic framing. However, the paper does NOT isolate optimization settings as a variable — it does not compare decompilation accuracy on optimized vs. unoptimized bytecode. The 0.82 semantic similarity ceiling could stem from many factors beyond optimization (e.g., model capacity, training data coverage). Sample size (9,731 functions in test set) is adequate but evaluation focuses on decompilation quality broadly, not ABI recovery specifically.

### 1.2 Wang et al. (2025) — "Building Reuse-Sensitive Control Flow Graphs (CFGs) for EVM Bytecode" (full text)
arXiv:2505.14437, no DOI.

This paper is the most detailed documentation of how compiler-introduced transformations create structural ambiguities in EVM bytecode. It identifies **8 distinct code reuse patterns** generated by compilers:

> "Code reuse occurs when two distinct execution paths share basic blocks (BBs) in bytecode, despite having disparate contexts. It is generated by compilers to compress smart contract size."

**Specific findings:**
- Compiler-introduced code reuse creates *fake join nodes*, *fake loops*, *sequences of fake join nodes*, and *nested fake loops* — all of which mislead static analysis tools into seeing control-flow dependencies that do not exist semantically.
- Even Gigahorse, the leading research decompiler, fails to eliminate code reuse in 11.17% of generated CFGs (11,769 polymorphic jump targets across 1,106 CFGs).
- Pattern matching is "inherently restricted by the influence of the compilation environment" in complex reuse scenarios.
- Esuer (the paper's proposed tool) achieves 99.94% trace coverage but requires *dynamic* identification of reuse contexts through stack emulation — static pattern matching alone is insufficient.
- Dataset: 10,000 popular smart contracts, 252,726,569 historical transactions, 950,221 execution traces.

**Quality assessment:** Moderate-to-strong. The paper provides granular, experimentally validated evidence that compiler transformations create intractable ambiguities for static analysis. However, it addresses CFG construction, not ABI/function signature recovery directly. The connection to H1 is that if even CFGs (a more basic primitive) are corrupted by optimization, then dispatch-pattern detection (PUSH4/EQ sequences) — which relies on correct control-flow — is undermined. The 11.17% failure rate of Gigahorse on code reuse represents a concrete lower bound on the optimization-induced degradation.

The paper's acceptance at this venue suggests the problem of compiler optimization impacting signature recovery is recognized as important and non-trivial by expert reviewers. We do not know whether it finds that optimization defeats recovery, or that recovery can overcome optimization. A paper titled "When X Meets Y" could argue either direction. Marked as **insufficient evidence** for building the proponent case.

### 1.4 SCDBench (Qin et al., 2026) — "SCDBench: A Benchmark for LLM-Based Smart Contract Decompilers" (full text)
arXiv:2605.29059, no DOI.

This paper provides an **upper-bound ceiling** argument for H1. It evaluates frontier LLMs (Claude Opus 4.7, GPT-5.3-Codex, GLM-5) on the end-to-end task of bytecode-to-Solidity decompilation with ABI recovery as one of four evaluation stages.

**Specific findings:**
- The best model (GPT-5.3-Codex with compilation repair) achieves ABI micro-F1 of **0.896** across 600 contracts — not 1.0. The gap is substantial on hard contracts (F1 drops to 0.861).
- Perfect semantic consistency (all functions recovered, all checkpoints matched) is achieved on only **42/600 contracts** (7.0% for Opus 4.7, 6.8% for GPT-5.3-Codex).
- On hard contracts, **zero** models achieve perfect decompilation.
- These results hold despite: (a) models receiving EVM assembly as input, (b) optional function-selector hints being available, (c) a one-shot compilation-repair step.
- The benchmark spans compiler versions v0.4 to v0.8 and difficulty levels (200 easy, 200 medium, 200 hard), with bytecode sizes from 2,924 to 15,342 bytes median.

**Quality assessment:** Moderate. The 600-contract benchmark is well-constructed (stratified sampling, near-duplicate filtering, semantic checkpoints). However, the paper does NOT control for optimization settings as a variable. The observed ceiling (ABI F1 ≈ 0.90) could be due to LLM limitations, prompt engineering, or evaluation strictness rather than inherent optimization-induced information loss. The paper's own conclusion emphasizes "structural success is not semantic success" — this supports H1's framing but does not specifically attribute the gap to the `--optimize` flag.

### 1.5 SigRec (Chen et al., 2021) — "SigRec: Automatic Recovery of Function Signatures in Smart Contracts" (full text)
DOI: 10.1109/tse.2021.3078342.

This paper provides *partial* support for H1's mechanism claim while contradicting its central prediction. It documents specific cases where optimization destroys structural artifacts:

> "Such runtime bound check is not needed if the array index is a constant and the smart contract is compiled with optimization due to the compile-time bound checks." (§2.3.1)

**Mechanism evidence:**
- The paper documents that compiler optimization removes bound-check patterns (LT instructions) that SigRec's rules depend on for static array dimension inference in external functions. With optimization, these checks are eliminated for constant indices.
- Struct parameters are fundamentally indistinguishable from individual parameters: "there is no sufficient hint from the bytecode to distinguish these two different situations" — this is an *inherent* ambiguity, not one introduced by optimization but one that optimization cannot resolve.

**Counter-evidence to H1:**
- SigRec explicitly tests "with or without optimization" and finds "the accuracy never goes below 96% across all compilers (from V 0.1.1 to V 0.8.0) with or without optimization." (§5.3)
- Sample: 119,404 unique open-source smart contracts, 210,869 public/external functions.
- Method choice dominates: SigRec outperforms competitors by 22.5% to 80.5%, vastly exceeding any optimization-induced variance.

**Quality assessment:** Strong. This is the most rigorous direct test of H1's central claim. The paper's experimental design explicitly included optimization level as a variable. Result: optimization does NOT dominate accuracy. For the proponent case, the mechanism evidence (bound-check removal, struct ambiguity) is valid but the headline result is counter-evidence.

### 1.6 Neural-FEBI (He et al., 2023) — "Neural-FEBI: Accurate function identification in Ethereum Virtual Machine bytecode" (abstract only)
DOI: 10.1016/j.jss.2023.111627.

**Available evidence from abstract:**
> "it is challenging to identify functions from these stripped contract binaries due to the lack of internal function call statements and the compiler-inducing instruction reshuffling."

The abstract acknowledges compiler-induced instruction reshuffling as a challenge for function identification. Neural-FEBI achieves F1-scores of 88.3–99.7 for function entry identification across 38,996 publicly available smart contracts.

**Quality assessment:** Weak. Abstract-only. We do not know whether the paper tested optimization effects specifically, whether the performance varies by optimization level, or what the mechanism of "compiler-inducing instruction reshuffling" is. The 88.3% lower bound suggests some contracts are difficult, but we cannot attribute this to optimization.

### 1.7 EthIR (Albert et al., 2018) — "EthIR: A Framework for High-Level Analysis of Ethereum Bytecode" (full text)

This paper acknowledges, in its introduction, that compiler optimizations affect analysis of bytecode-level artifacts. It provides no experimental evidence on ABI recovery specifically — the paper is about decompilation into a rule-based representation (RBR) for resource analysis.

**Quality assessment:** Very weak for H1. A single sentence of acknowledgment without experimental backing. Not usable as primary evidence.

## 3. THE MECHANISM — HOW H1 WOULD BE TRUE

The hypothesis identifies a plausible causal chain:

**Step 1: The optimizer applies non-injective transformations.** Constant folding replaces expressions with pre-computed values (e.g., `PUSH4 0xa9059cbb` after folding replaces a sequence of arithmetic opcodes). Dead code elimination removes unreachable dispatch branches. Expression simplification merges distinct patterns into equivalent forms. David et al. (2025, full text) explicitly names these three transformations.

**Step 2: These transformations destroy PUSH4/EQ dispatch patterns.** The Solidity compiler generates function dispatch as comparing `PUSH4 <selector>` against the calldata prefix followed by `EQ` and conditional `JUMPI`. When the optimizer folds constants, removes dead paths, or merges comparisons, the PUSH4/EQ/JUMPI triple that static analysis depends on may be transformed, reordered, or eliminated. Wang et al. (2025, full text) documents related compiler transformations (code reuse patterns) that create structurally misleading CFGs.

**Step 3: The lost signal is information-theoretically irrecoverable.** David et al. (2025, full text) provides the entropy framing: bytecode (6.30 bits/opcode) is denser than Solidity (4.22 bits/token). The compilation+optimization process is a lossy compression — function names, type names, comments are discarded. The optimizer further removes the redundancy that decompilers use as signal. Multiple distinct source programs can map to identical optimized bytecode, making perfect recovery mathematically impossible.

**Step 4: No method fully compensates.** SCDBench (2026, full text) shows that even frontier LLMs with compilation repair achieve ABI F1 of at most 0.896 and perfect semantic consistency on only 7% of contracts. SigRec (2021, full text) notes inherent ambiguities (struct vs. individual parameters) that cannot be resolved from bytecode alone regardless of optimization.

## 4. HONEST ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORTING CASE STRENGTH

### What the evidence supports:
- **Compilers DO transform bytecode structure in ways that complicate static analysis.** This is supported by David et al. (2025) and Wang et al. (2025), both full-text papers with substantial empirical evidence.
- **An information-theoretic ceiling exists** — compilation is lossy, and optimization makes it more lossy. SCDBench provides a concrete upper bound (ABI F1 ≈ 0.90 for frontier LLMs, 7% perfect semantic recovery).
- **Some bytecode patterns ARE destroyed by optimization** — SigRec documents bound-check removal and struct/individual ambiguity.

### What the evidence does NOT support:
- **The claim that optimization DOMINATES ABI recovery accuracy.** The single most rigorous test of this hypothesis (SigRec, 119,404 contracts, explicit optimization-variable testing) finds: (a) accuracy is >96% with OR without optimization, (b) the gap between methods (SigRec vs. competitors: 22.5–80.5 percentage points) dwarfs any optimization effect.
- **The claim that no method can fully compensate.** SigRec achieves 98.7% accuracy — and while not 100%, the residual error is attributable to inherent ambiguities (structs, fixed-size vs. dynamic arrays), not optimization-specific information loss. SCDBench's LLM results (ABI F1 0.896) are dominated by model choice (0.896 vs. 0.697 vs. 0.428), not by compilation settings.

### Where the case rests on abstracts alone:
- The most potentially supportive paper — Lin & Gao (2021), IEEE S&P, "When Function Signature Recovery Meets Compiler Optimization" — is available only as abstract-only (and the corpus captured no abstract text). This paper could provide the strongest direct evidence for H1, but we have **zero findings** from it. Its relevance is purely speculative based on its title.
- Neural-FEBI's evidence is similarly thin: abstract-only, mentions "compiler-inducing instruction reshuffling" as a challenge but provides no data on optimization-specific effects.

### Overall verdict:
The proponent case for H1 is **weak by the standards of the evidence corpus**. The central prediction — that optimization settings dominate ABI recovery accuracy — is directly tested and contradicted by the highest-quality available evidence (SigRec, full text, large sample, controlled variable). The mechanism is partially valid (optimizations do destroy some patterns), but this does not translate to a performance ceiling, because other bytecode features (parameter-handling semantics, calldata access patterns, masking operations) are robust to optimization and provide sufficient signal for near-perfect recovery. The hypothesis conflates the loss of *one specific* signal (PUSH4/EQ dispatch patterns) with a loss of *all* recoverable signal — a conflation contradicted by the evidence.

The strongest remaining argument for H1 is the **information-theoretic** one: compilation is mathematically lossy, and no method can achieve 100% perfect ABI recovery from bytecode alone (SigRec's 98.7% and SCDBench's ABI F1 0.896 support this ceiling). But whether this ceiling is *dominated by* optimization settings rather than by other factors (inherent type ambiguities, contract complexity, model capacity) is a claim for which the evidence tilts strongly negative.
